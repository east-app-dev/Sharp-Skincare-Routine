Does this product fit my skin?

How to read a skincare label, match a cleanser, moisturizer or sunscreen to your skin type, and test it without wrecking your skin.

By Esat Karabiyik, SharpUpdated 2026-11-214 min read
TL;DRKnow your skin type first. Then check texture, key claims (non-comedogenic, fragrance-free, broad-spectrum SPF) and the first ingredients. Patch-test new products and add one at a time.

Step 1: know your skin type

A product is only “good” relative to your skin. Oily skin wants light, oil-free textures; dry skin needs richer creams; sensitive skin needs fewer, gentler ingredients. If you’re not sure, a face scan or a simple test helps: wash, wait an hour without products, and see where you get shiny or tight.

Step 2: read the label

Label claimUseful for
Non-comedogenic / oil-freeOily and breakout-prone skin
Fragrance-freeSensitive skin, razor irritation
Broad-spectrum SPF 30+Everyone, every morning
Rich cream, ceramides, sheaDry skin
Salicylic acidClogged pores, blackheads

Ingredients are listed roughly by amount, so the first few matter most. Alcohol high on the list can sting dry or freshly shaved skin.

Step 3: check the role in your routine

A great serum is useless if you’re missing a cleanser or sunscreen. Fill the basic steps first, then add extras one at a time.

Step 4: patch test and give it time

Try a new product on a small area for a few days before using it on your whole face. Then give it 4 to 8 weeks, unless it burns, itches or causes a rash; stop then.

FAQ

What does non-comedogenic mean?

That the product is formulated not to clog pores. It’s useful guidance for oily or breakout-prone skin, though not a guarantee.

Can a moisturizer be too rich for my skin?

Yes. Creams designed for very dry skin can feel heavy on normal or oily skin and may contribute to clogged pores.
