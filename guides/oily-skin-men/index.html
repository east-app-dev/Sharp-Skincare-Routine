Oily skin in men: what actually helps

Why your face gets shiny by noon, what makes it worse, and a simple routine that keeps oil and breakouts in check.

By Esat Karabiyik, SharpUpdated 2026-11-215 min read
TL;DRWash twice a day and after sweating with a gentle foaming cleanser, still moisturize with an oil-free product, use oil-free SPF, and consider salicylic acid. Don’t scrub harder or skip moisturizer; both tend to backfire.

Why skin gets oily

Oil (sebum) comes from sebaceous glands. How much you make depends mostly on genetics and hormones, plus heat and humidity. Men tend to have more active oil glands, which is why the T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) often looks shiny by midday.

Oil isn’t the enemy. It protects the skin, but too much can clog pores and lead to blackheads and breakouts.

A routine for oily skin

  1. Cleanse morning, night and after exercise with a gentle foaming or gel cleanser. Salicylic acid cleansers can help if you also get blackheads or breakouts.
  2. Moisturize with an oil-free, non-comedogenic product. Skipping moisturizer can leave skin irritated and doesn’t reliably reduce oil.
  3. Use an oil-free sunscreen (SPF 30+) or a moisturizer with SPF in the morning.
  4. Blot during the day with blotting papers instead of washing again and again.

Common mistakes that make it worse

  • Washing too often or with harsh soap and hot water.
  • Scrubbing hard or using rough exfoliators every day.
  • Heavy, greasy creams and hair products that run onto the forehead.
  • Touching your face or picking at spots.
  • Switching products every few days, so nothing gets time to work.

Ingredients worth knowing

IngredientWhat it does
Salicylic acidOil-soluble exfoliant that helps clear clogged pores
NiacinamideCommonly used to support the skin barrier and reduce the look of shine
Benzoyl peroxideTargets breakout-causing bacteria; can dry and bleach fabric
RetinoidsHelp keep pores clear over time; start slowly, use at night

Track it, don’t guess

Shine changes with weather, sleep and stress, so the mirror is a bad judge. Sharp scores oil and sebum as one of six measures every week, so you can see whether a new cleanser or moisturizer is actually moving the number.

If you have painful, deep or persistent breakouts, see a board-certified dermatologist; prescription treatment works far better than any cosmetic routine for that.

FAQ

Should I moisturize oily skin?

Yes. Use a light, oil-free, non-comedogenic moisturizer. Skipping it can irritate the skin.

How often should I wash an oily face?

Twice a day and after sweating. More often tends to irritate the skin.

Does oily skin age slower?

Oily skin often shows fine lines later, but sun protection matters far more for skin aging than skin type.
